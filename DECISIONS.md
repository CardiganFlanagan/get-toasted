Week 10 (Nov 2 – Nov 8) · Assignment 10: Three Enemy Types
Three enemies, three jobs. The chaser (ant) walks at you and bites on contact. The beetle charger winds up for 0.6 s (red blinking) and then dashes. The bee shooter keeps its distance, aims for 0.7 s (a dotted line to the player) and fires a stinger. Each one asks the player for a different reaction: keep moving, sidestep, close in. For the shooter role I picked a bee (it replaced the mold from my first enemy design).
One pattern for the state machines: enum State + match state + change_state(new_state, time) with one countdown timer. The beetle and the bee each have 4 states (Chase, Windup/Aim, Dash/Shoot, Cooldown), and I can explain both scripts the same way. The chaser has no state machine: it follows the toast on the navmesh, bites on contact and stands still while the bite animation plays. If it needs three states too, the plan is Chase → Bite → Cooldown with the same change_state().
Pathfinding and sight: every enemy uses NavigationAgent2D on one NavigationRegion2D, so they walk around obstacles instead of getting stuck on them. The beetle dashes only when the player is closer than vision_range and a RayCast2D with force_raycast_update() hits the player first. The bee shoots only when its raycast reaches the player. Walls block both. The beetle has no angle check (field of view) yet: inside vision_range it sees in every direction.
Week 11 (Nov 9 – Nov 15) · Assignment 10: review and playtest
AI review of the enemy commit (Claude): the beetle was on the walls' collision layer (layer 2, mask 5). It dashed through walls, its raycast saw the player through walls, and the bee's raycast treated the beetle as a wall. I moved it to the enemy layer (layer 4, mask 3), like the other enemies.
Old duplicate: shooter.tscn is an early copy of bee.tscn without NavigationAgent2D, so it would crash if spawned. No wave in LevelData uses it. To do: delete it.
Week 11 (Nov 9 – Nov 15) · Assignment 11: Data and Saves
No balance numbers in scripts. Custom Resources: EnemyStats (one class, @export_group sections for the beetle and bee extras), PlayerStats, WeaponStats and LevelData with an Array[Wave]. Every enemy, the toast and the weapon read their numbers from a .tres. Only the current HP lives on the node.
Three levels from data. level_1..3.tres are the shifts Завтрак (45 s, 4 waves), Обед (60 s, 5 waves) and Ужин (90 s, 6 waves). One level.gd loads levels[Game.level_index]. Winning a shift unlocks the next one.
Saves. The Game autoload writes user://save.json (version, unlocked, best_time, best_score, music, sounds). I did not use .tres for saves, because a resource file can carry a script. Safe load: a missing, empty or broken file gives the defaults, and JSON numbers come back as float, so they are turned back into int. Each save goes to save.tmp first and is then renamed. Saves happen at the end of a run, on a volume change and when the app is paused or closed, never every frame. Reset progress asks for confirmation in a ConfirmationDialog.
(1) Its install steps did not say to close Godot. The open editor saved project.godot again and dropped the Game autoload, so I got Identifier "Game" not declared. The fix was to add Game back in Project Settings → Autoload, and the install steps now say to close Godot first. (2) The first version kept volume in settings.cfg and spawn points as Marker2D nodes in level.tscn, but the brief wants settings in the save file and spawn points in LevelData. Volume moved into save.json. I kept spawn points in the scene, because they depend on the arena layout and all three shifts use the same arena.
